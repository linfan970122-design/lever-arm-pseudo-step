**Definitions** (identical to `analysis/align_lidar_events.py`)

* Narrow window = `[t − Δt − 0.15, t + 0.15]` s, i.e. the RTK jump interval padded by 0.15 s.
  Δt is the RTK message interval of the event (0.2 s at 5 Hz).
* `Δψ_lidar` = sum of frame-to-frame point-to-plane ICP yaw increments over that window,
  strictly frame-to-frame, no accumulation.
* `Δψ_gyro` = trapezoidal integral of CH110 gyro-z over the **same** window.
* `Δψ_lidar(±1 s)` and `n(±1 s)` use `[t − 1.0, t + 1.0]` s (the paper's wide window;
  there is no ±3 s field in the data — ±3 s was only the ICP *computation* span).
* ICP noise floor σ = 0.1037° per frame (median of the per-bag quiet-frame RMS, `summary.md` (a));
  3σ = 0.3111°.
* `lidar vs RTK` = Δψ_lidar / Δψ_RTK (signed ratio). Negative ⇒ opposite direction.
* Fitness = Open3D ICP inlier fraction, RMSE in metres; both reported as
  min/median (fitness) and max/median (RMSE) over the frames in the narrow window.

## 3. The 12 events above the 3σ floor — one verdict line each

Two independent tests per event:

* **gyro agreement** — |Δψ_lidar − Δψ_gyro| ≤ 2·3σ = 0.622°. Pass ⇒ the lidar saw a
  *real* vehicle rotation (two independent sensors agree). Fail ⇒ ICP artefact.
* **explains the RTK step** — same sign **and** |Δψ_lidar| ≥ ½|Δψ_RTK|.

A third column, **gyro itself above floor** (|Δψ_gyro| > 3σ), separates a genuine small
turn from a case where the gyro says the vehicle was still and only the lidar moved.

### Which criterion picks the Table-5 4 out of the 12

Table 5 is generated in `align_lidar_events.py` by the **magnitude-only** rule

```
abs(dpsi_lidar) > 0.5 * abs(dpsi_rtk)   AND   abs(dpsi_lidar) > 3*sigma
```

Summary of the sensitivity test:

* Above-floor events that stay above the floor at every shift: **10 / 12**.
* Above-floor events for which **some** shift would let the lidar rotation explain the
  RTK step (same sign, ≥ ½ magnitude): **2 / 12**.
* Below-floor events (63) that any shift would push above the floor: **10**.
* Largest |Δψ_lidar| span across the five window positions, over the 12 above-floor
  events: 17.799°; median span 2.521°.

Interpretation, stated against the numbers above and not beyond them:

* Δψ_lidar is **not** insensitive to where the window is cut. Median span across the
  five positions is 2.521°, maximum 17.799°. On the small events the window slides over
  ICP noise and simply resamples it; on the genuine turns it slides over a real,
  continuing rotation, so the sum grows or shrinks with the window position.
* The **floor test is mostly robust**: 10 of 12 above-floor events stay above the floor
  at every shift, and 2 (16:59:36, 09:50:26) drop below it at some shift. In the other direction, 10 of
  the 63 below-floor events would rise above the floor at some shift — so the 63/12 split
  is itself accurate only to about ±10 events, and the paper should not present 63 as exact.
* The **conclusion is nearly but not fully shift-invariant**: at the paper's window 1 of 75 events pass
  sign-and-half-magnitude; allowing any of the four shifts raises that to 2
  (1 above-floor + 0 below-floor events gain the verdict at some shift).
  The above-floor one is 17:13:52: Δψ_lidar = -0.911° at the paper's window but 8.895° at +0.5 s,
  i.e. the window slid onto the start of a later genuine turn. That is a statement
  about window placement, not evidence that the RTK step was real.

## 5. Sentences the counts support

Of the 75 pseudo-step events with lidar coverage:

* 63 (84%) have |Δψ_lidar| below the 3σ ICP noise floor of 0.311°;
* 67 (89%) below 1°; 70 (93%) below 2°;
* 12 are above the floor. Of those, 10 are **corroborated real rotations** (lidar and
  gyro agree within 2·3σ **and** the gyro itself exceeds 3σ); 1 agree with a gyro that
  is itself below the floor, i.e. no rotation is corroborated; 1 disagrees with the gyro
  and is ICP residual rather than motion.
* **1 of 75** events have a lidar rotation that could explain the RTK heading step
  (same sign and at least half the magnitude).

🔴 **This count is 1, not 0.** The exception is 2026-08-28 17:09:37: RTK stepped +6.194°, lidar
  +3.4870°, gyro +3.3390° — same sign, 56% of the step magnitude. The vehicle really
  was turning, and the lidar rotation covers over half the RTK step, so this one event
  is **not** demonstrated to be a pseudo-step by the lidar evidence. It is one of the
  Table-5 four. Any sentence claiming "none" or "no event" is wrong; the correct
  claim is that 74 of 75 are contradicted and 1 is ambiguous.

A defensible replacement for the Fig. 9 title, e.g.:

> On 74 of the 75 lidar-covered pseudo-step frames the measured vehicle rotation cannot
> account for the RTK heading step (median step 13.1°): 63 frames show no rotation above
> the ICP noise floor, and the 11 that do rotate turn by at most 17.69° or in the opposite
> direction to the step.
